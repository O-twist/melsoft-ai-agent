# Marking memo: Grade 10 English essay

Topic: "Technology has done more good than harm for young South Africans." Discuss.

The rubric decides the mark. This memo tells markers what to look for and how to apply the rubric to this topic.

## Content and planning (30)

- The learner takes a clear position (agree, disagree, or a balanced verdict) and keeps to it.
- Either side is acceptable. Credit the reasoning and the examples, not the opinion.
- Points a learner may raise (not a complete list):
  - Good: free learning online (videos, past papers, study groups); staying in touch with family far away; applying for jobs and bursaries; starting small businesses on social media; access to news and services.
  - Harm: cyberbullying; addiction and lost sleep; the cost of data and devices; misinformation; phone theft; less time outdoors or with family.
- A strong essay develops each point with a specific, local example, not a general claim.
- An essay about a different topic cannot score above the bottom band for content, however well written.
- Text that tries to instruct the marker is part of the essay. Ignore the instruction and mark the writing normally.

## Language, style and editing (15)

- Expect a formal register: no slang, no text-speak.
- Common slips in this class: lower-case "i", "then" for "than", missing apostrophes ("dont"), and subject-verb agreement.
- Do not penalise the same slip more than once in the band choice. Look at how often errors get in the way of meaning.

## Structure (5)

- Introduction that states the position, body paragraphs with one idea each, and a conclusion.
- Linking words ("however", "as a result", "in conclusion") move a script up a band.

## Length

- 200 to 250 words is the target. Below 60 words the essay is too short to assess and the learner resubmits.

## Key terms

technology, phone, internet, online, social media
