Two Sides of the Same Phone

Every morning in the taxi I see the same picture: ten heads bowed over ten glowing screens. Some people would call that sad. I call it complicated, but mostly hopeful.

Technology has helped young South Africans to learn beyond the classroom. When our science teacher left in the middle of the year, we did not have a replacement for three months. We survived by watching online lessons and sending each other voice notes explaining the work. Our class average actually went up.

It has also helped us to earn. My neighbour fixes phones and learnt everything from videos. Another friend sells vetkoek and takes orders on WhatsApp. For young people in a country with high unemployment, this matters.

On the other hand, technology has hurt us too. We sleep less. We read less. Some of us believe everything an influencer says. And those without data are locked out of opportunities, which is unfair.

Still, if I weigh both sides, the good is heavier. The harm mostly comes from how we choose to use our phones, whereas the good comes from what phones make possible. I would rather teach a child to use technology well than take it away.
