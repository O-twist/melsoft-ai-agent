Technology: A Ladder, Not a Trap

When my grandmother was sixteen, the nearest library was a two-hour walk from her village. Today I carry more books in my pocket than that library ever held. For young South Africans, technology has done far more good than harm, because it has opened doors that distance and poverty once kept shut.

The clearest benefit is access to learning. During load-shedding and teacher shortages, free video lessons and past papers kept many of us on track. A learner in Giyani can now watch the same maths explanation as a learner in Sandton. That does not erase inequality, but it narrows the gap.

Technology has also created work. Young people sell clothes on social media, design logos for clients overseas, and code apps that solve township problems. These are jobs our parents could not have imagined.

Of course there are dangers. Cyberbullying wounds deeply, and endless scrolling steals hours we will never get back. Yet these are problems of how we use the tool, not of the tool itself. A ladder can be misused too, but nobody blames the ladder.

Technology is not perfect, but it has given my generation something priceless: a fair chance to climb.
